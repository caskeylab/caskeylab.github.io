Research

Our research addresses a central challenge in therapeutic ultrasound: how to precisely deliver, measure, and interpret acoustic interactions with living tissue, especially in the brain.

Program areas

1. Focused ultrasound neuromodulation

Focused ultrasound can excite, inhibit, or modulate neural activity without implants or incisions. We study neuromodulation across scales, from cellular responses to distributed brain networks.

  • Circuit-level neuromodulation in nonhuman primates using MRI guidance and simultaneous fMRI.
  • Pulse repetition frequency and temporal structure as determinants of neuronal response.
  • Mechanistic studies linking acoustic parameters to cellular and network-level outcomes.

2. Image-guided acoustic therapy and safety

Precise therapy requires knowing where ultrasound energy is delivered and what it does to tissue. We develop imaging-based methods to localize acoustic fields and assess exposure.

  • MR Acoustic Radiation Force Imaging for nonthermal localization of focused ultrasound beams.
  • Integration of MRI, optical tracking, and simulation for targeting and validation.
  • Quantitative assessment of heating, displacement, and cavitation risk.

3. Imaging, measurement, and ground-truth validation

Ultrasound–tissue interactions are physical processes that must be quantified before biological interpretation. We treat measurement as a first-class scientific objective.

  • Cross-validation using MR thermometry, displacement imaging, acoustic simulation, and biological readouts.
  • Experimental paradigms designed to avoid measurement artifacts and confounds.
  • Linking pressure, displacement, and timing to biological responses observed optically or with fMRI.
